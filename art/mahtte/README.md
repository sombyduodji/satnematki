# Player characters

Everything here is generated by [`tools/make_mahtte.py`](../../tools/make_mahtte.py). Change the
script, not the images, so a correction reaches every view and frame.

The characters are 3D models, looked at pixel by pixel and lit in three flat bands of the same colours as before.

| Path | Contents |
|---|---|
| `base/<gákti>-<boy\|girl>-views.png` | each view of the character: front, lower right, side, upper right and back |
| `base/hair-options.png` | every hair choice on the first gákti |
| `base/animations-<boy\|girl>.png` | the plain animations |
| `sprites/<gákti>-<gender>-<style>.png` | the 36 sprite sheets the game uses |
| `mouths.js` | where the mouth is in each frame, for opening it while the character talks |

Hair and irises in a sheet are in key colours, which the game swaps for the player's choice as it loads the sheet: `HAIR_RAMPS` and `EYE_RAMPS` in `src/game.js`.
The eyes are bigger than a real child's so that their colour can be seen. The eye colours are the user's choice and include pink and lilac on purpose.

Each gender has its own nine hairstyles, numbered in the chooser: `HAIR_STYLES` in the script and `STYLES` in `src/game.js`.
Boy: short, tousled, parting, swept, crop, fringe, mid, lowtail, curls. Girl: short, tousled, mid, long, braids, bun, ponytail, bob, curls. They are general styles, not based on any source.
Hair colour is a slider, `HAIR_RAMPS` in `src/game.js`.

Sprite frames are 24×40 of the world's pixels, drawn at four sheet pixels to each, so 96×160. Two rows lie side by side in a sheet. Rows: idle down, up and right, walk down, up and right (eight frames each),
then cheer, jump, crouch and fall (six frames each, facing the player). Left is right mirrored.

Rows 10–33 repeat idle and walk for each direction with the hem and hair ends moved -2, -1, 1 and 2.
Rows 34–35 are idle down and up with the hem lifted. `cloth` in `src/game.js` hangs the hem and hair on a
spring and picks the row each frame, so they trail behind a walk,
swing more slowly than the legs, and carry on a little when the character stops or turns.

Rows 36–55 are the diagonal views: idle lower right, idle upper right, walk lower right, walk upper right, each
with sway -2, -1, 0, 1 and 2. The other two diagonals are these mirrored. 

Rows 101–103 are work to the right, for the lávvu camp: chopping, sawing and throwing, six frames each. The tool is drawn by `src/camp.js`.

Rows 56–60 are the jump: rising, top and coming down, facing down, up, right, lower right and upper right. Row 61
is getting up after a fall and brushing the gákti; row 9, the fall, leads into it.

Rows 62–96 are running, for the classroom: facing down, up, right, lower right and upper right, each with sway -3 to 3.
Row 97 is the slide on the tummy, head first to the right: the dive, three frames sliding, lying still, and pushing up.
Row 98 is sitting on a chair, side on: going down, then six frames with the lower legs swinging off the seat's edge.

## Classmates

The classmates in `art/classmates/` come from the same model, with the same detail, as the player's characters: `CLASSMATES` in the script, and `python3 tools/make_mahtte.py classmates` makes only them. Each sheet has the poses of a lesson: the hand going up on the first row and sitting on the second. Two wear the player's gákti, one of them with a blue shawl that has no source, and the rest have generic everyday clothes, for the user to check.

## Everyone else

The store's customers and staff and the teachers come from the same model too, with their looks in `PERSON_LOOKS` in `src/models.js`: `python3 tools/make_mahtte.py people` makes them into `art/animals/`, with what they carry as pictures of their own. Grown-ups have longer bodies and smaller heads than the children.

## The gákti

The design follows Store norske leksikon's article
[Karasjok-kofta](https://snl.no/Karasjok-kofta). It says children's clothes are miniatures of adults'.

**Both:** blue cloth; red neck and sleeve edges; red chest cloth split at the neck opening, edged in blue,
with narrow yellow and red strips below; *hárdodilli* (red and yellow) across the back; *dearis* (red) down
the back seams to the belt; nebbsko (*bievssut*) with woven shoe bands, white with red and blue.

**Boy:** high *čeabe* collar, red with yellow and red strips; silk *čeabetliidni* under the neck inside the kofta, which the article says can
vary in colour, here white as the user asked; short, with no *holbi*, only a red double seam at the hem; leather trousers (*sisttehat*). The narrow
white scarf crossed over the chest is described only for a groom, so it isn't used here.

**Girl:** no separate collar, because the article describes only the red cloth in the neck opening; reaches
below the knee; yellow strip, red gathered *holbeláđđi* and a lower edge (*holberavda*); stockings. A big silk shawl (*liidni*) over the shoulders, with
a deep fringe and a round silver brooch, as the user asked: the article gives silk in various colours and patterns, fringes in shades of those, and brooches.
How it's folded and the colours here are a choice, for the user to check.

**Belt:** Kárášjohka has the woven belt, white with a red pattern and red and blue edges. Ohcejohka has
the leather belt with silver buttons.

## To check

- **The two designs have no place names for players** (user's decision, 2026-11-03). The chooser shows them as
  1 and 2, and they differ only in the belt, a documented option and not a known Utsjoki feature. `ohcejohka`
  and `karasjohka` are internal names. Background, if the designs are ever changed, from the sources:
  - SNL: the Karasjok kofta is also worn in Utsjoki. Women's koftas from Utsjoki and Karigasniemi have less
    gathering at the lower edge, and the women's hat is somewhat lower. Local differences are evening out.
  - [Sámi Duodji](https://www.samiduodji.com/18): the Utsjoki dress is the least decorated in Finland, worn
    along the whole Teno on both sides of the border. Blue is most common; black, dark blue and white were
    common when it was sewn from sarka.
  - Siida museum records marked Ohcejohka/Utsjoki: [boy](https://www.finna.fi/Record/musketti_siida.M030:S%C3%812729:1)
    (blue; the red chest cloth continues into the collar; narrow yellow and red strips under the collar),
    [girl](https://siida.finna.fi/Record/siida.sda-20918) (black; hem blue at the bottom, then red, with a
    yellow seam), [woman](https://siida.finna.fi/Record/siida.sda-22565) (black; hem dark red, then red,
    with a yellow seam). These are single garments, not rules.
  - [Áile Aikio](https://faktalavvu.net/2018/02/27/gakti-sukujen-puku/): the gákti follows the family, not
    the place, and the upper and lower Teno differ.
- **Belt buttons:** Finnish Wikipedia says square buttons mark married couples, while SNL says Karasjok men's
  buttons don't differ by marital status. The base model draws square buttons, on a child. Check this.
- **Hem edge colour** on the girl's gákti: the article gives the width of the *holberavda*, not its colour.
- **The 3D redrawing** (2026-11-05): the same parts as before, in the same places as far as a round body allows. Where the parts meet the shoulders and round the body is new and has no source. The girl's gathers on the *holbeláđđi* are no longer drawn as lines.
- **Everything else**, by someone who knows these clothes.
